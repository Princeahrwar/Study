# Interviews: Track Plan

> **Track code:** INT · **Weeks:** story bank from week 5, resume in week 13, mocks from week 14, interview mode in weeks 24–26 · **Budget:** ~25 hours · **Prerequisites:** none; this track turns the others into offers

Knowing the material is half of an interview. The other half is showing it inside 45 minutes to someone deciding your level: telling stories that demonstrate scope, structuring answers so the signal is easy to see, and having rehearsed under pressure. This track collects everything that is about the interview itself rather than the subject: how each company runs its loop, your stories, your resume, mock interviews, and the facts you should have at instant recall.

---

## 1. What this track gives you

1. **Optional notes per company**, for the companies you are actually interviewing with: the loop format, what each round tests, the signals that set your level, and the tutorials to revise first. The rest of the track does not depend on them.
2. A **story bank** of about ten stories, each told at three lengths, mapped to the behavioural signals interviewers score.
3. A **resume** rewritten around measured impact and the six projects.
4. A **mock schedule and rubric**, so practice is scored the way the real loop is.
5. **Flashcards** for the numbers and facts that must come instantly.

Interview pipelines take five to ten weeks from application to offer, which is why applications start in week 14 while study continues.

## 4. Behavioural signals

Whatever a company calls them, behavioural rounds at Senior and Staff level score a handful of signals:

| Signal | What the interviewer looks for |
|---|---|
| Ownership and depth | You can go deep on what you personally built and debugged, and you are honest about what you did not do |
| Handling ambiguity | You scope a vague problem, decide with incomplete information, and say what you assumed |
| Leading without authority | Working across teams; influencing a decision you do not own |
| Conflict and feedback | Disagreeing well, receiving direct feedback, changing your mind for a reason |
| Impact and scale | Outcomes with numbers, who was affected, the trade-offs you chose |
| Learning from failure | A real mistake, what it cost, and what changed so it cannot recur |
| Customer and production focus | Incidents, escalations, delivery across teams |

The story bank maps each story to these signals so you can pick the right one in the moment. If you are interviewing somewhere in particular, read the company's own published values as well.

---

## 5. How to study this track

1. Write each story in full once, then practise telling it, never reading it.
2. Record at least one mock a week and watch it back; count filler words and check whether your first sentence answered the question.
3. Score every mock against the rubric and log the result; the log shows trends that a single mock hides.
4. Use paid or peer mocks with real interviewers for coding and design from week 14 (for example interviewing.io, Hello Interview, or peers from your network).
